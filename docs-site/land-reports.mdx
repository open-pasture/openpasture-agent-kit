# Land Reports

A land report is what is known about a place. The engine asks for one for the
herd's current paddock and each candidate before every decision.

## What you ask for

`get_land_report` takes a GeoJSON geometry and the sections you need. Anything
not requested is not fetched.

| Section | What comes back |
| --- | --- |
| `weather` | Current conditions, history, and forecast. |
| `imagery` | Latest satellite capture, NDVI stats, and history. |
| `climate` | Drought history and precipitation and temperature normals. |
| `hazards` | Flood, fire, and severe weather history. |
| `soil` | Soil survey data. |
| `vegetation` | Native, invasive, and plants toxic to livestock. |
| `species` | Wildlife, pests, and pollinators. |
| `water` | Surface water, wetlands, and floodplain. |
| `terrain` | Elevation, slope, and aspect. |

A `Point` is valid too; the report then covers a buffer around it. Coordinates
are `[longitude, latitude]`.

## What you get back

Each section comes back with its data and its sources. A section that could
not be filled returns `status: "unavailable"` with a reason. It is never
silently dropped.

The land connector stores each section as an observation linked to the land
units it covers, keeping the `report_id` and sources as provenance. A decision
lists the `land_report_ids` it used. Reports are cached by geometry, section,
and time, so repeated decisions do not refetch unchanged data.

## Providers

| Provider | Use it for |
| --- | --- |
| Alexandria | Real data for your land. Needs `FIRECRAWL_API_KEY`. |
| Open data | The fallback when no key is set. Fills weather; imagery comes back unavailable. |
| Fixtures | Offline work, demos, and tests. Returns canned reports, not your land. |

Pick with `OPENPASTURE_LAND_SOURCE` (`auto`, `alexandria`, `open_data`, or `fixture`). See
[Environment Variables](/environment-variables).

<Warning>
  Fixture reports look real but describe no actual place. Do not make grazing
  calls on real animals from fixture data.
</Warning>

## Reading a report well

- Check the imagery capture date and cloud cover before trusting forage
  estimates.
- NDVI is a proxy for green cover, not grass height.
- A fresh field note beats imagery when the two disagree.
- Name unavailable sections to the farmer instead of guessing around them.
